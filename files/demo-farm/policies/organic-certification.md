# Organic certification -- what we have to do

Owner: Sarah | Last review: 2026-02-22 (annual cycle)

Hollow Creek Farm is certified under USDA Organic, administered by
NOFA-NY Certified Organic LLC. Cert ID HCH-2019-117. Our cert
covers all vegetable production, the dairy operation, and the
processing facility (small).

This document is the operational summary of what the cert demands of
us. The cert standards themselves are in `policies/source/nop-rule-cfr-7-205.pdf`
and `policies/source/nofa-ny-standards-2025.pdf`.

## What "organic" requires of us, in practice

### Vegetables

- **No prohibited substances**, period. Synthetic fertilizers,
  synthetic pesticides, GMO seed. Any input must be on the OMRI
  list OR be specifically approved by NOFA-NY in writing.
- **Documented crop rotation** -- ours is in
  [crops/rotation-schedule](/89416d7d-940e-4d04-94e2-0f9be1dd09b1). Minimum
  3-year rotation between same-family crops; we run 4.
- **Buffer zones** -- 25 feet from any conventional neighbor's
  field. We have one such border (Ranney's, west side of North-A);
  the buffer crop is rye, not for sale.
- **Cover cropping** -- some form of soil cover for every field
  that's bare > 30 days outside winter.
- **Seed sourcing** -- organic seed where commercially available.
  When not, we document the search (3 vendors checked) and use
  untreated conventional. See `crops/seed-sourcing-log.md`.

### Dairy

- **Organic feed only** -- since 12 months before first milk shipment.
  All cows in the herd are bought organic-from-birth or transitioned
  with documented 12-month feed history. See
  [feed suppliers](/dc003350-f249-48f5-8a5c-372a85c1f7c0).
- **Pasture rule** -- minimum 120 days/year on pasture, minimum
  30% dry matter intake from pasture during grazing season. We
  track DMI roughly through milk yield drop on rainy weeks (rough
  proxy) and through pasture rotation records.
- **No prohibited antibiotics or hormones.** A cow treated with a
  prohibited substance leaves the organic herd permanently. We've
  had one case (Bramble #102, mastitis 2022, decided not to treat
  with conventional antibiotics, used alternative protocols
  including dry-off; she recovered).
- **Living conditions** -- access to outdoors, fresh air, direct
  sunlight, room to lie down, fresh water.

## The annual cert cycle

| Month | What |
|---|---|
| December | Annual update form to NOFA-NY: changes to operation, new fields, new equipment, new buyers. |
| January | Renewal fee due ($895 for our scale, 2026). Self-assessment due. |
| February | NOFA-NY assigns inspector. We pick a date. |
| April–May | On-farm inspection. Inspector spends 4-6 hours. |
| June | Inspector report comes back. Address any "minor non-compliance" findings (we've had 0–2 each year). |
| July | Cert renewed for the next 12 months. |

## What triggers a complaint or de-certification

- Detected residue of a prohibited substance on a sample.
- Failure to document an input.
- Buyer complaint that leads to investigation.
- Conventional neighbor spray drift across our buffer with no
  reporting.

We have never had any of these. The closest call was a 2021 buffer
infraction (Ranney's spray drift during a 30mph westerly), which we
reported ourselves; NOFA-NY confirmed our reporting and no penalty.

## Why we do it

Cert costs us about $2,400/year in fees and probably $5,000/year in
extra documentation labor. The price premium on dairy alone covers
it 4x over. The CSA program is harder to argue numerically -- many
of our members would buy from us cert or not -- but the cert is
table stakes for the wholesale account at Greenstar Co-op.
