# Four-year crop rotation

Owner: Pat | Last review: 2025-11-12

Four fields rotated through four crop groups in a strict cycle. The
goal is to break pest and disease cycles, balance soil nutrient draw,
and keep the soil covered through the off-season.

## The cycle

```
Year 1: Solanaceae        (tomatoes / peppers / eggplant / potatoes)
Year 2: Cucurbits         (squash / cukes / melons)
Year 3: Brassicas + alliums (broccoli / cabbage / kale / leeks / garlic)
Year 4: Roots + legumes   (carrots / beets / peas / beans + cover crop)
```

After year 4, the field's cover-crop residue is incorporated and the
cycle restarts at Solanaceae.

## Cover crops between cash crops

- After Solanaceae (Sep/Oct): rye + hairy vetch, terminated mid-May
- After Cucurbits (Aug/Sep): oats + peas, winter-kill
- After Brassicas (Oct): cereal rye, terminated April
- After Roots+Legumes (no cover, plowed under bean residue, then
  garlic planted Oct in same field)

## Soil tests

Annual fall test on each field. Results archived in
`crops/soil-tests/<year>/`. Looking for:

- Organic matter ≥ 4% (currently 4.2–5.1% across the four fields)
- pH 6.2–6.8
- P, K within recommended range for upcoming crop group

## Why the strict rotation matters

Beyond the organic-cert requirement (NOFA-NY requires documented
crop rotation), a four-year gap between Solanaceae plantings is what
keeps verticillium wilt and late blight populations low in the soil.
We skipped this once in 2020 (compressed to 2 years because of a
land lease change) and paid for it for the next three seasons.
