# Blight watch -- May 2026

**Alert level: YELLOW.** Last updated: 2026-05-21 by Pat.

Late blight (_Phytophthora infestans_) is the standing risk for our
tomato (North-A, 3.5 ac) and potato plantings. The May rain
forecast (NWS Ithaca, 7-day): 1.4" expected, with three consecutive
days of >85% RH. That puts us above our action threshold.

## Current status of plantings

- **Tomatoes (North-A):** 480 transplants in the ground since May 8.
  Heirlooms (Brandywine, Cherokee Purple, Black Krim) -- no genetic
  resistance. Pastes (Amish Paste, Roma) -- partial tolerance only.
- **Potatoes (South-B):** 1,800 sq ft, planted Apr 22. Yukon Gold
  and Adirondack Red, hilled once on May 14.
- **Volunteer potatoes (everywhere):** dug out 4 patches already, two
  more spotted in North-B yesterday. Will revisit Thursday.

**Three of four triggered.** We are at the level where reactive
copper applications become reasonable, but we are deliberately
holding off on the first one until we see actual lesions OR the PA
report becomes a confirmed late blight (vs. early blight; awaiting
cultivar ID).

## Field inspection routine

Daily, 7am, until conditions clear:

1. Walk North-A in five transects, looking specifically at the
   lower canopy of the heirloom rows.
2. Photograph any suspect lesion -- water-soaked, no halo, gray-green
   margins. Compare against
   [Cornell IPM gallery](https://www.vegetables.cornell.edu/pest-management/disease-factsheets/late-blight/).
3. Pull and bag (not compost) any plant with confirmed late blight.
4. Log finding in this document under "Observations."

## If late blight is confirmed

1. Pull and destroy (burn or deep-bury) the infected plant + the
   four nearest plants.
2. Apply copper hydroxide (Champ WG, OMRI-listed, allowed under our
   organic cert). 1.5 lb / acre. Re-apply every 5-7 days under
   pressure.
3. Notify Devon to keep dairy stock out of the affected field
   (compost residue concern).
4. File a regional report on USABlight.org -- it helps everyone in
   the watershed.

## Why this doc lives at YELLOW even when symptoms are zero

Late blight goes from "first lesion" to "field destroyed" in 5-7
days under right conditions. Reactive-only treatment is the strategy
that kills farms. We document the watch state because next year, when
we go to read this back, we want to remember WHY we held off vs.
WHY we sprayed.
