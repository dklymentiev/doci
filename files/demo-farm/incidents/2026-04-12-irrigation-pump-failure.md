# Incident -- irrigation pump failure, April 12 2026

Owner: Mike | Status: resolved | Reviewed: Sarah, Mike, Pat 2026-04-20

## Summary

The well pump feeding North-A irrigation failed Saturday April 12 at
~2:40pm during the first full-pressure pre-season test. Replacement
unit installed April 14. No crop loss (test was pre-planting). Two
days of unplanned downtime. Total cost: $3,820 (pump $2,950, labor
$640, expedited delivery $230).

## What happened

Pat opened the North-A drip header to test pressure ahead of
tomato transplanting. Pressure climbed from 0 to ~25 psi in 4
minutes, then stalled. Within 30 seconds the pump made a grinding
noise, then went quiet. The breaker had tripped.

We reset the breaker. The pump motor turned for ~10 seconds, then
tripped again. Suction line full of water, no obstruction. Pump
case felt hot.

We called Drilling Done Right (DDR), our well company. They came
Sunday morning. The pump (Goulds GT15, installed 2019) had a seized
bearing on the impeller side. The motor had been straining against
it for some time, likely all of the 2025 season.

## Root cause

The bearing failure was the symptom. The cause was that we didn't
notice progressive wear because we don't do a daily pressure check
during winter (the pump is off most of December–March) and the
spring start-up "ran fine" because there was no load.

DDR estimated the bearing had been failing slowly since at least
mid-2025. Pat noticed a slight whine in August 2025 but it was
intermittent and not noticeable under load.

## What we changed

- Added daily pressure check at North-A drip header to the
  [morning checklist](/9d988b73-5cb5-41a0-8156-04025931bb43),
  running May–September.
- Added 30-day post-install inspection for the new pump (June 1,
  2026) to the [equipment log](/a2ffac6c-7506-4ec3-8a73-e896d1e65424).
- New rule: ANY whine or grind from any pump, fan, or motor is a
  log entry in the handoff, even if it goes away. We caught the
  August 2025 whine and dismissed it. We won't dismiss the next one.
- Asked DDR to send a quote for a parallel backup pump (cost ~$1,800
  for an unconnected redundant install). Decision pending; if the
  next failure had been in July with crops in, we'd have lost real
  revenue.

## What didn't go wrong (worth noting)

- The pump failed during a test, not under load with a crop
  dependent on it.
- DDR responded within 24 hours despite being a weekend.
- The replacement unit was in stock at the regional distributor.
- Total downtime: 48 hours. Acceptable for our pre-season window.

## What we'd do better

- Don't run the first pressure test alone. Two people on scene
  means faster diagnosis and someone to call DDR while the other
  is at the pump.
- Schedule the pre-season pressure test at least one week before
  the first transplanting window. We had four days of buffer; we
  used three.
